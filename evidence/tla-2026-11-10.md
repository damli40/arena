# TLA+ results: the guard's double-pay race, model-checked with near-tla (2026-11-10)

Plan, written before any TLC run: `evidence/tla-plan.md`. Code: `tla/` (`Guard.tla`, `run.sh`).

## Results

**Both modes came out as planned.** `vulnerable_fixture` lets the receiver get 200 on a 100
invoice. `fixed` never does within the bounds, **provided** each transfer settles before
`reconcile` reads the balance (WaitRule) **and** nothing but the guard moves the guard's
tokens. Drop either condition and `fixed` pays 200 on the 100 invoice (`mut_no_wait_rule`;
Finding 3 below, review H1). `./run.sh` ran all 17 checks, all matched, exit 0, about 27 s wall
time. (Nov 11: now 19 checks, all as planned; see "Corrections after adversarial review".)

| check | mode | bounds (invoices / pays / reconciles / forced failures) | result | distinct states | states generated | depth | TLC time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vuln_race` | vulnerable_fixture | 1 / 2 / 0 / 0 | **NoOverpay violated** (as planned) | 31 | 40 | 8 | <1 s |
| `vuln_testnet_order` | vulnerable_fixture, testnet order | 1 / 2 / 0 / 0 | **NoOverpay violated** (added, see Deviations) | 24 | 30 | 8 | <1 s |
| `fixed_1inv` | fixed | 1 / 3 / 1 / ≤1 | **pass** (as planned) | 9,408 | 25,777 | 14 | <1 s |

| `fixed_1inv_2faults` | fixed | 1 / 3 / 1 / ≤2 | **pass** (added, see Deviations) | 24,120 | 69,423 | 17 | 1 s |

"Distinct states" is how many different situations of the whole system (guard, token, receipts
in flight) TLC found. "Depth" is the number of steps in the longest of the shortest paths to
them. Forced failures can hit `ft_transfer` (the token transfer) or `on_transfer` (the guard's
callback). The pass counts are the same on every run. The violation counts vary by a few states
between runs, because TLC's 4 worker threads stop at slightly different points.

What these numbers decide: **assuming** each transfer has settled before `reconcile` reads the
balance, and nothing else moves the guard's tokens, the fixed guard's two checks ("not Paid",
"not Pending", and the `Pending` write in the same step) hold in every order of up to 3 `pay`
calls, 1 `reconcile` and 1 failure on 2 invoices, or 2 failures on 1 invoice (2 failures was
run with 1 invoice only). The code enforces 100 blocks, not settlement; without the assumption
the fixed guard pays twice (Finding 1). With one outside transfer into the guard it also pays
twice (Finding 3). The whole check costs seconds, so the bounds could be raised later without a
budget problem.

**Anti-vacuity checks: 8 witnesses reached, 3 of 3 mutants caught.** So the pass is not
coming from "nothing interesting happens" inside the model. The witnesses cannot show what the
model leaves out (Finding 3, review H1).

### Findings worth carrying forward (two on Nov 10, a third added Nov 11)

1. **The reconcile waiting rule is load-bearing.** Without it the fixed guard pays twice, and
   no failure is needed, only a slow transfer. The trace (`mut_no_wait_rule`):

   | step | what runs | invoice | receiver |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1-3 | agent signs `pay`; owner signs `reconcile`; agent signs a second `pay` | Approved | 0 |
   | 4 | `pay` #1 runs: marks `Pending`, starts the transfer | Pending | 0 |
   | 5-6 | `reconcile` runs; the token reports the guard's balance: 1000, so the money has not left yet | Pending | 0 |
   | 7 | the first transfer lands | Pending | **100** |
   | 8 | `on_reconcile` runs: the balance it read equals the ledger, so it concludes "the money did not leave", sets `Approved` | Approved | 100 |
   | 9-10 | `pay` #2 passes both checks and its transfer lands | Pending | **200** |

   The epoch check in `on_reconcile` (lib.rs:273-276) does not stop this, because the token
   transfer does not change the guard's epoch. Only the transfer's callback does, and it has
   not run yet. The code's own comment already says the waiting rule "is a waiting rule, not a
   proof" (lib.rs:40-43). The model cannot check the rule as time, because near-tla receipts
   have no deadline (its SEMANTICS.md, N-time and N-congestion: "a finding about latency bounds
   needs the real scheduler"). So this is a **hypothesis**: it needs a transfer delayed by
   100+ blocks, for example by congestion. I have not replayed it in near-contract-sim or the
   sandbox.

2. **At one failure, reconcile's "money did not leave" branch cannot be reached** while the
   waiting rule holds. An invoice gets stuck `Pending` only if its callback failed, and the
   money is still there only if the transfer failed too. That takes two failures. So
   `fixed_1inv`'s pass said nothing about that branch; `fixed_1inv_2faults` covers it, and
   it passes.

3. **(Added 2026-11-11, review H1.) `reconcile` pays twice if anyone else sends tokens to the
   guard.** This one needs no slow transfer: the waiting rule is ON. It needs one dead
   callback and one ordinary token transfer into the guard from another account, for example
   the owner topping it up. Check `h1_outside_transfer_reconcile` (`fixed`, 1 invoice, 2 pays,
   1 reconcile, WaitRule TRUE, forced failures on `on_transfer` only, at most 1,
   `Donate = TRUE`): **NoOverpay violated**, 4,033 distinct states, 10,329 generated, depth 14,
   about 1 s. In plain words:

   1. The agent's first `pay` runs. Its transfer **succeeds** (Sam has 100, the guard 900), but
      its callback dies (out of gas, or the fault build). The invoice is stuck `Pending`.
   2. The owner tops the guard up by the invoice amount, 100. The guard holds 1000 again. The
      guard is not told; its ledger still says 1000.
   3. The owner calls `reconcile`. It reads the balance, sees "balance = ledger", decides "the
      money did not leave", and sets the invoice back to `Approved`.
   4. The agent pays again. The check passes, the transfer lands: **Sam has 200 on the 100
      invoice.**

   TLC's trace (`python3 -I trace.py build/h1_outside_transfer_reconcile/tlc.out`; step 9 is the
   outside account's transfer being signed, which `trace.py` prints without a label):

   ```
    5. ran pay (tx 2) -> created ft_transfer, on_transfer           | Pending  sam:0   ledger=1000
    6. ran ft_transfer (tx 2)                                       | Pending  sam:100 ledger=1000
    7. FORCED FAILURE of on_transfer (tx 2)                         | Pending  sam:100 ledger=1000
    8. ran reconcile (tx 1) -> created ft_balance_of, on_reconcile  | Pending  sam:100 ledger=1000
    9. (outside account signs ft_transfer(guard, 100), tx 4)        | Pending  sam:100 ledger=1000
   10. ran ft_transfer (tx 4)                                       | Pending  sam:100 ledger=1000
   11. ran ft_balance_of (tx 1)    -> reads 1000                    | Pending  sam:100 ledger=1000
   12. ran on_reconcile (tx 1)                                      | Approved sam:100 ledger=1000
   13. ran pay (tx 3) -> created ft_transfer, on_transfer           | Pending  sam:100 ledger=1000
   14. ran ft_transfer (tx 3)                                       | Pending  sam:200 ledger=1000
   => invariant NoOverpay violated in the last state
   ```

   Where it lives in the code (`contracts/guard/src/lib.rs`):
   - `reconcile` is owner-only (`:231`). So the person who must not top up during the stuck
     window is the same person who calls `reconcile`.
   - The owner cannot re-sync the ledger while an invoice is `Pending`: `sync_ledger` refuses
     (`:314`). So after a top-up there is no way to correct the ledger before reconciling.
   - The decision is balance equality alone (`:282-293`): "balance = ledger − amount" means
     Paid, "balance = ledger" means Approved. Any untracked inflow shifts the balance. Any
     inflow since the last `sync_ledger` counts, not only one made during the stuck window.

   How likely: it needs a dead callback first (the callback has no panic path, so in practice
   out of gas), then an inflow exactly equal to the stuck amount. An owner topping up "the 100
   that looks missing" is the natural way to hit it.

   Status: this check is **expected to fail** and is in `run.sh` as a must-fail. It records the
   bug in the current guard. Dami chose to fix `reconcile`; that is a separate change to
   `contracts/guard`, after which this check's expectation should flip to `pass`. **Not done:**
   no replay on the real Rust source or the sandbox. The trace is model-only.

## The counterexample, in plain words (`vulnerable_fixture`)

**The testnet order** (`vuln_testnet_order`: TLC restricted to orders where no transfer runs
until both `pay` calls have been signed and checked, the order seen on testnet):

| step | what runs | invoice status | receiver (Sam) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | start: invoice approved for 100, guard holds 1000 | Approved | 0 |
| 2 | agent signs `pay` (call A) | Approved | 0 |
| 3 | agent signs `pay` (call B) | Approved | 0 |
| 4 | **call A checks** "not Paid": passes, starts transfer A, records nothing | Approved | 0 |
| 5 | **call B checks** "not Paid": passes (still Approved), starts transfer B | Approved | 0 |
| 6 | transfer A lands | Approved | 100 |
| 7 | transfer B lands | Approved | **200** |

NoOverpay breaks at step 7, before either callback has run. Both callbacks would later mark
the invoice `Paid` twice.

**The shortest order** (`vuln_race`, no restriction): pay A runs, transfer A lands, *then* pay B
runs and its transfer lands: 200. Same length (7 states). It shows the window is wider than
"both in one block": a second `pay` passes as long as it runs before the first callback.

**Checked against testnet** (`evidence/testnet-2026-11-09.md`, rows 1-5, vulnerable_fixture):
both calls were signed at block N, **both `pay` checks ran in block N+1**, both transfers in
N+2, both callbacks in N+3 (row 1: 276973000 → 276973001 → 276973002 → 276973003, the same for
A and B). Sam got 200 in 5 of 5 trials. That is the model's testnet-order trace step for
step: check A, check B, transfer A, transfer B, then the callbacks. The model has no blocks, so
this matches the **order**, not the block numbers.

`fixed`: in the same situation, call B's check sees `Pending` and fails with "payment already
in flight" (lib.rs:184). The testnet fixed trials refused the second call with the same message.

Each check is this TLC call, in `build/<check>/` with its generated `Guard.cfg`:

```sh
/opt/homebrew/opt/openjdk@21/bin/java -XX:+UseParallelGC -Xss16m -Djava.io.tmpdir=<dir>/jtmp \
  -DTLA-Library=tla/vendor/near-tla/modules -cp tla/vendor/tla2tools.jar tlc2.TLC \
  -deadlock -workers 4 -metadir <dir>/states -config Guard.cfg Guard.tla
```

## Deviations from the plan

1. **Witness `reconcile_says_approved` was not reached at 1 forced failure.** The plan listed
   that as a surprise. The reason is finding 2 above (it needs two failures). I added
   `fixed_1inv_2faults` (2 failures) after seeing this. It passes, and the witness is reached
   there. `run.sh` records the miss as its own check (`wit_reconcile_says_approved_1fault`,
   expected "not reached") so that the 1-failure result stays visible.
2. **Added `vuln_testnet_order`**, not in the plan, to check the trace against the testnet
   order. TLC's shortest trace had pay B after transfer A, which is valid but is not the order
   testnet showed. **My first version of this filter was wrong.** It blocked a transfer only
   while a signed `pay` was waiting, so a transfer could still run before call B was signed. One
   run happened to print the testnet order anyway; a later run printed "transfer A, then sign
   B". I tightened it to "no transfer until every `pay` has been signed and checked"
   (`TestnetOrder` in `Guard.tla`), re-ran it 3 times plus the full suite, and every trace now
   has both checks first. The numbers above are from after the fix.
3. Otherwise as planned: the same bounds, `fixed_2inv` finished in 9 s (no need to lower it),
   and the same invariants, witnesses and mutants.

## Limits

**What near-tla's conformance covers, and what it does not.** near-tla's README and
CONFORMANCE.md say its gas and receipt-outcome rules were compared with nearcore 2.14.0's
`Runtime::apply` through near-contract-sim ("every one of 210,283 gas splits ... matched
exactly"; I did not run that suite). **Receipt order is not part of that comparison.** Its
ordering rules rest on targeted simulator tests and on reading nearcore. This model runs in
fault mode, so gas does not appear in it. What it uses from near-tla is mainly the receipt
order and the failure semantics (a failed receipt reverts only itself; its callback still
runs, and sees Failed). So the conformance-checked part (gas) is the part this model does
**not** depend on.

**Model versus code.** `Guard.tla` is written by hand from `lib.rs`. A pass is about the model.
Nothing here checks the compiled wasm. The only link to the real contract is that the
vulnerable counterexample matches what the compiled guard did on testnet (above). The fixed pass
and the `no_wait_rule` trace have not been replayed on the compiled contract.

**Not modelled (in the original Nov 10 runs):**
- **Other accounts sending tokens to the guard** (added Nov 11, review H1). In every original
  check only the guard's own transfers move tokens (`Holders == {G} ∪ receivers`, and `Next`
  has no outside transaction). The "two stories" logic of `reconcile` is only sound in that
  closed world. `Donate = TRUE` now adds this; see Finding 3.
- `approve_invoice` and `sync_ledger` as steps (invoices are approved and the ledger is synced
  at the start); `transfers_started`, `confirmed_payouts`, logs.
- Time. The reconcile waiting rule (`RECONCILE_MIN_BLOCKS`, 100 blocks) is an assumption,
  `WaitRule`: reconcile runs only once the `pay` that made the invoice `Pending` has nothing in
  flight. Finding 1 shows the fix depends on it.
- Gas. Fault mode only: "out of gas" is represented by forcing a receipt to fail, which has the
  same effect on state (no writes).
- The token is near-tla's standard NEP-141, not Arena's `contracts/test_token`. If `test_token`
  differs from the standard (in what `ft_transfer` returns, or when it panics), the model does
  not see it.
- Shards, blocks and latency: near-tla allows any order the causal links allow. That is wider
  than a real network, so it is safe for a pass (orders the network never produces are checked
  too). It also means a counterexample may need an unusual delay (finding 1).
- Each invoice has its own receiver, so a receiver's balance equals what that invoice paid. Two
  invoices to one receiver are not modelled.
- `#[private]` callbacks: modelled as "fails if the caller is not the guard", but the model never
  generates an outside caller, so that check is never exercised.
- The `FailedStaysPayable` exception for "callback died" is tracked per invoice and cleared when
  `on_reconcile` settles it. With at most 2 failures this cannot excuse a second, unrelated
  stuck state, but I did not prove that for larger bounds.

**Not run.** near-tla's own runner (`tools/run.py`, including `--anti-vacuity` and its audit of
gas, storage and log edges). The brief allowed running only the TLA+ checker from downloaded
repositories. So the witnesses and mutants are hand-written in near-tla's style and run
through TLC directly. near-tla's audit, which would list receipts never pushed to their edges,
was not produced.

**Bounds.** Up to 3 `pay`, 1 `reconcile`, 2 failures, 2 invoices (never all at their maximum
in one run: 2 invoices was run with 1 failure). Nothing is claimed beyond them.

**Trust list.** TLC 2.19 and Java 21; near-tla at `dd40301` (repository created Oct 24 2026, one author,
per the research note); nearcore 2.14.0 / protocol 88 as near-tla encodes it; my reading of
`lib.rs` into `Guard.tla`.

## Corrections after adversarial review (2026-11-11)

Source: `evidence/proofs-adversarial-review-2026-11-11.md`. Nothing above was silently rewritten:
each changed sentence is listed here with what it said before.

**What changed my mind.** The review added one action the model did not have, an outside
account sending 100 tokens to the guard, and TLC found a double pay in the `fixed` guard with
the waiting rule ON (H1). And the headline stated the pass without the WaitRule condition it
rests on (H2). Both make the Nov 10 headline wrong as written.

| where | said before | now says |
|---|---|---|
| Results, first paragraph | "`fixed` never does, within the bounds, including with forced failures and `reconcile`." | `fixed` never does, provided each transfer settles before `reconcile` reads the balance and nothing else moves the guard's tokens; without either it pays 200 on 100 (H1, H2) |
| "What these numbers decide" | "hold in every order of up to 3 `pay` calls, 1 `reconcile` and 2 failures" | adds the two assumptions; says 2 failures was run on 1 invoice only (2 invoices: 1 failure) |
| Anti-vacuity paragraph | "So the pass is not coming from 'nothing interesting happens'." | adds "inside the model. The witnesses cannot show what the model leaves out (H1)." |
| "Not modelled" | had no line about outside transfers | adds "Other accounts sending tokens to the guard" |
| Findings | two findings | Finding 3 = H1, with the trace |

**Model changes** (`tla/Guard.tla`, `tla/run.sh`). Two new constants, both `FALSE` in every
original check, so those checks see the same model:
- `Donate`: adds an outside account `donor` holding 100 tokens and 1 yoctoNEAR, and one action
  `DonTx` (it calls the token's `ft_transfer(guard, 100)` once, at any time). The NEAR
  conservation check uses `NearG + 1` when it is on.
- `GhostRec`: records which invoices `on_reconcile` set back to `Approved` (ghost `recApp`).

**Re-run, Nov 11** (`tla/run.sh`, exit 0, **19 of 19 as planned**). The four pass checks gave
the same counts as on Nov 10: `fixed_1inv` 9,408, `fixed_2inv` 239,994, `fixed_1inv_2faults`

nothing when they are off. Violation and witness counts moved by a few states, as the Results
